================================================================================
PART 1 — THE PROBLEM (IN DETAIL)
================================================================================

MOIL Limited is India's largest manganese ore producer, operating multiple mines
in the Nagpur-Bhandara-Balaghat belt of central India (Maharashtra and Madhya
Pradesh). Manganese is a critical mineral — it is essential for steel production,
battery technology, and numerous industrial applications. India is one of the
world's top manganese producers, and MOIL alone accounts for roughly 50% of the
country's manganese output. Any inefficiency at MOIL directly impacts the
national supply chain.

MOIL faces two interconnected, critical challenges:


CHALLENGE 1: EXPLORATION UNCERTAINTY
-------------------------------------
Finding new manganese deposits is extraordinarily difficult. The traditional
approach relies on:

  - Manual geological field surveys: Geologists physically walk the terrain,
    collect rock samples, and visually inspect outcrops. This is slow (weeks to
    months per area), expensive (requires teams, equipment, travel), and covers
    limited ground.

  - Random or experience-based drilling: Without precise targeting, companies
    drill exploratory boreholes at locations chosen by intuition or rough
    geological maps. Each borehole costs Rs 15-30 lakh (roughly Rs 1.5-3 million),
    and the success rate of hitting an economically viable deposit is often
    below 20%.

  - Under-utilization of satellite data: India has access to vast amounts of
    free satellite imagery (Sentinel-2, Landsat, etc.) and geospatial data
    (geological maps from GSI Bhukosh, elevation data from SRTM). These
    datasets contain signatures that correlate with manganese deposits —
    specific spectral indices (Iron Oxide Index, Clay Mineral Index) visible
    from space, vegetation patterns (NDVI), terrain features (elevation, slope),
    and geological structures (faults, shear zones). Despite this, most mining
    companies including MOIL do NOT systematically use AI/ML to combine these
    data sources and generate predictive prospectivity maps.

The result: MOIL spends crores of rupees on blind exploration, drills in
locations that yield nothing, disturbs forest ecosystems unnecessarily, and
misses high-potential zones hiding in plain sight in satellite data.


CHALLENGE 2: PRODUCTION SHORTFALLS & FLEET INEFFICIENCY
--------------------------------------------------------
Even at existing, operational mines, MOIL faces significant production
challenges:

  - Reactive management: Production plans are set monthly, but disruptions
    (monsoon rains, equipment breakdowns, road deterioration) are handled
    reactively — AFTER they cause damage. By the time a shortfall is detected,
    tons of ore have been lost and targets missed.

  - Monsoon impact: During the 4-month monsoon season (June-September), heavy
    rainfall degrades haul roads, reduces equipment availability, limits
    blasting days, and slows dumper movement. Production can drop 20-40% in
    these months, and mines scramble to compensate.

  - Fleet misallocation: MOIL operates fleets of dumpers (ore-carrying trucks)
    and shovels (excavators) at each mine. The assignment of "which dumper goes
    to which shovel" determines throughput. Sub-optimal assignment — too many
    dumpers at one shovel (causing queuing), too few at another (causing idle
    time), assigning a broken dumper — leads to wasted fuel, idle equipment
    costs (Rs 5,000/hour per idle dumper), and missed production targets.

  - No predictive warning system: Mine managers lack a system that predicts
    shortfalls BEFORE they happen and recommends corrective actions (deploy
    backup fleet, fix road, reroute dumpers) proactively.

The result: Production shortfalls cost MOIL an estimated 5-15% of planned
output annually, translating to crores of rupees in lost revenue, wasted diesel,
and idle equipment costs.


================================================================================
PART 2 — THE SOLUTION: MOIL-GeoSync
================================================================================

MOIL-GeoSync is an integrated AI-powered platform that addresses BOTH problems
with two complementary AI sub-modules, unified in a single interactive Streamlit
dashboard:


MODULE 1: GeoProspect AI (Exploration)
---------------------------------------
A machine-learning-based mineral prospectivity mapping system that combines
satellite imagery with geological data to predict where manganese deposits are
most likely to be found.

HOW IT WORKS:
  1. Fetches real Sentinel-2 satellite imagery from Microsoft Planetary Computer
     (or falls back to realistic synthetic data when real imagery is unavailable)
  2. Computes spectral indices: NDVI (vegetation), Iron Oxide Index (laterite/
     manganese signature), Clay Mineral Index (alteration zones)
  3. Combines spectral data with geological features: rock type, fault distance,
     shear zone proximity, elevation, slope, rainfall, soil moisture
  4. Uses a Positive-Unlabeled (PU) Bagging Random Forest — a specialized ML
     algorithm designed for cases where you only have "known deposit" labels
     (positives) and "unknown" labels (not necessarily negatives). Standard
     classification would treat unknowns as negatives, biasing the model.
     PU Bagging corrects this by bootstrap-sampling from the unlabeled data.
  5. Validates with Spatial Block Cross-Validation — prevents spatial data
     leakage where nearby points in train/test sets inflate metrics artificially
  6. Applies NDVI vegetation masking — reduces confidence in predictions under
     dense forest canopy (Sal/Teak forests) where satellite spectral signatures
     are obscured by vegetation
  7. Outputs a prospectivity score (0.0 to 1.0) for each grid point, classified
     as High (>0.8), Medium (0.4-0.8), or Low (<0.4)

KEY METRICS (ILLUSTRATIVE):
  - 2,500 grid points analyzed at ~1 km resolution
  - F1 Score: 0.91 (Spatial Block Cross-Validation)
  - Precision: 0.85 | Recall: 0.79


MODULE 2: MineFlow Optimizer (Production)
------------------------------------------
An AI-driven production forecasting and fleet optimization system that predicts
shortfalls and generates optimal dumper-shovel dispatch plans.

HOW IT WORKS:
  1. Uses a Gradient Boosting Regressor (150 estimators, max_depth=5) trained on
     historical production data: planned production, rainfall, equipment
     availability, blasting days, haul road condition, crusher capacity, fleet
     size, and 3-month lag features
  2. Predicts actual production for upcoming months and flags shortfall risk:
     High (predicted/planned < 90%), Medium (< 95%), Low (>= 95%)
  3. Feeds predictions into a Mixed-Integer Linear Programming (MILP) optimizer
     using Google OR-Tools (SCIP solver) to generate optimal dumper-to-shovel
     assignments that maximize throughput while respecting constraints:
       - Each available dumper assigned to exactly one shovel
       - Maximum 4 dumpers per shovel
       - Shovel throughput cannot exceed shovel capacity
       - Total daily output cannot exceed crusher capacity
       - Weather penalties reduce effective dumper capacity
       - Road condition penalties reduce effective dumper capacity
       - Equipment availability determines which dumpers are active
  4. Generates automated alerts: CRITICAL (throughput <90% of plan), WARNING
     (heavy rain, poor road), INFO (dumper unavailable for maintenance)

KEY METRICS (ILLUSTRATIVE):
  - R2 = 0.90 | MAE = 112.5 TPD | RMSE = 145.2 TPD
  - 193 optimized fleet dispatch assignments with 74 automated alerts
  - 5% shortfall reduction across 3 mines

BACKEND PIPELINE SCRIPTS (src/)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These scripts form a staged pipeline that must be run in order before launching
the dashboard. Each script produces data files that the next one consumes.


3.1  src/fetch_real_data.py — STAGE 1: Satellite Data Acquisition
-----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  - Builds a regular latitude/longitude grid (0.01 degree spacing, ~1 km) over
    the study area: 21.0-21.5 N, 79.0-79.5 E (Nagpur-Bhandara-Balaghat belt)
  - Connects to Microsoft Planetary Computer's STAC API (zero authentication,
    free, open-access satellite data catalog)
  - Searches for recent Sentinel-2 Level-2A scenes with <20% cloud cover
  - Downloads 5 spectral bands (B02-Blue, B04-Red, B08-NIR, B11-SWIR1,
    B12-SWIR2) as Cloud-Optimized GeoTIFFs streamed via HTTP
  - Samples band values at each grid point by transforming lat/lon to the
    scene's native UTM coordinate system
  - Computes 3 spectral indices from the raw bands:
      NDVI = (B08 - B04) / (B08 + B04)  -> vegetation density
      Iron Oxide Index = B04 / B02       -> laterite/iron-bearing surfaces
      Clay Mineral Index = B11 / B12     -> clay-altered zones
  - If the real fetch fails (network, partial tile coverage), falls back to
    generating synthetic spectral data with realistic distributions modeled
    after actual Sentinel-2 values for the Deccan Plateau

OUTPUT: data/real_spectral.csv

WHY IT IS IMPORTANT:
  Satellite spectral indices are the foundation of mineral prospectivity
  mapping. NDVI reveals where vegetation is too dense to see the ground (low
  confidence zones). Iron Oxide Index and Clay Mineral Index are direct proxies
  for the surface expressions of manganese-bearing formations. Without this
  data, the entire exploration AI module would have no remote sensing input.
  The dual real/synthetic approach ensures the prototype always works — judges
  can see the pipeline in action even without internet access, while the real-
  data path proves the architecture is production-ready.


3.2  src/generate_data.py — STAGE 2: Training Dataset Generation
----------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  - Loads the spectral data from Stage 1 (data/real_spectral.csv)
  - Augments it with synthetic geological features:
      rock_type: Assigned based on lat/lon rules simulating GSI Bhukosh data
                 (5 types: Mn_bearing_schist, Laterite, Gondwana_sediment,
                 Deccan_basalt, Granite_gneiss)
      fault_distance_km: Random 0-30 km (simulating distance to nearest fault)
      shear_zone_proximity_km: Random 0-20 km
      elevation_m: Random 200-600 m (Deccan Plateau range)
      slope_deg: Derived from elevation with noise
      rainfall_mm: 800-1600 mm with latitude gradient
      soil_moisture: Derived from rainfall
  - Creates PU (Positive-Unlabeled) labels: ~15% of points labeled as
    "mn_occurrence = 1" based on favorable conditions (right rock type, high
    iron oxide, high clay, close to faults). 10-15% label noise injected to
    prevent artificially perfect model performance.
  - Generates a vegetation mask (NDVI > 0.7)
  - Creates the production dataset: 60 months (Jan 2021 - Dec 2025) of
    simulated operational data for 3 mines (Dongri Buzurg, Chikla, Munsar),
    including planned/actual production, rainfall, equipment availability,
    blasting days, haul road condition, crusher capacity, fleet counts
  - Generates lag features (lag_1, lag_2, lag_3) for time-series awareness
  - Computes shortfall risk categories based on actual/planned ratio
  - Creates a 12-month production forecast dataset for 2026

OUTPUTS:
  - data/prospectivity_dataset.csv (with labels, for training)
  - data/prospectivity_grid.csv (without labels, for prediction)
  - data/production_dataset.csv (historical production)
  - data/production_forecast.csv (future forecast template)

WHY IT IS IMPORTANT:
  This script is the data backbone of the entire system. It bridges the gap
  between raw satellite observations and ML-ready training data. The PU labeling
  strategy is critically important because in real-world mineral exploration,
  you know where deposits HAVE been found (positives), but you do NOT know that
  all other locations are deposit-free (they are "unlabeled", not "negative").
  Standard classification would treat unlabeled as negative, causing the model
  to miss potential deposits. The production dataset simulates realistic
  operational patterns including monsoon seasonality, equipment variability,
  and shortfall dynamics — enabling the forecasting model to learn real-world
  production patterns.


3.3  src/train_prospectivity.py — STAGE 3: GeoProspect AI Training
------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  - Loads prospectivity_dataset.csv
  - Encodes categorical rock_type using LabelEncoder
  - Implements the PUBaggingRF class (Positive-Unlabeled Bagging Random Forest):
      K=30 bootstrap iterations
      Each iteration: samples n_pos unlabeled examples, combines with all
      positive examples, trains a RandomForestClassifier (100 estimators)
      Final prediction: average probability across all 30 models
  - Validates using Spatial Block Cross-Validation:
      Creates spatial blocks by quantizing lat/lon to 0.1 degree bins
      Uses GroupKFold (k=5) so that nearby points are never split across
      train and test sets — prevents spatial autocorrelation from inflating
      metrics
  - Trains the final model on all data
  - Predicts mn_probability on the prospectivity_grid.csv
  - Applies NDVI vegetation masking: areas with NDVI > 0.7 get a 30% confidence
    penalty (score * 0.7), and are flagged as "Low (Veg)" confidence
  - Classifies scores into High/Medium/Low prospectivity classes
  - Saves the model ensemble as models/prospectivity_pu_rf.joblib

OUTPUT: models/prospectivity_pu_rf.joblib + updated prospectivity_grid.csv

WHY IT IS IMPORTANT:
  This is the core AI engine for exploration. The PU Bagging approach is
  specifically designed for mineral prospectivity mapping — a technique
  validated in peer-reviewed research (referenced in the README). Spatial Block
  CV is essential because geological data is spatially autocorrelated: nearby
  points are inherently similar. Without spatial blocking, a model that simply
  memorizes local patterns would score 0.99+ F1 but be useless for predicting
  new areas. The NDVI masking adds domain-specific intelligence — in the
  Nagpur-Bhandara belt, dense Sal/Teak forests obscure the ground surface,
  making spectral signatures unreliable. Reducing confidence in these areas
  prevents false-positive drill recommendations in forested zones.


3.4  src/train_production.py — STAGE 4a: Production Forecaster Training
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  - Loads production_dataset.csv (180 records: 3 mines x 60 months)
  - Defines 11 features: planned_production_tpd, rainfall_mm,
    equipment_availability_pct, blasting_days, haul_road_condition,
    crusher_capacity_tpd, num_dumpers, num_shovels, lag_1, lag_2, lag_3
  - Target: actual_production_tpd
  - Splits 80/20 train/test
  - Trains a Gradient Boosting Regressor (150 estimators, max_depth=5,
    learning_rate=0.1)
  - Reports MAE, RMSE, R2 on the test set
  - Predicts on the forecast dataset (production_forecast.csv) for 2026
  - Computes shortfall_risk for each forecast month
  - Saves the model as models/production_gb.joblib

OUTPUT: models/production_gb.joblib + updated production_forecast.csv

WHY IT IS IMPORTANT:
  This model enables PROACTIVE production management. Instead of reacting to a
  shortfall after it happens, mine managers can see predicted production for each
  upcoming month and identify high-risk periods (typically monsoon months) in
  advance. The lag features (lag_1, lag_2, lag_3) capture production momentum —
  if the last 3 months were bad, the next one is likely at risk too. Gradient
  Boosting was chosen because it handles non-linear interactions between
  features (e.g., the combined effect of heavy rain + poor road + low equipment
  availability) better than linear models.


3.5  src/optimize_fleet.py — STAGE 4b: Fleet Dispatch Optimization
------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  - Loads production_forecast.csv (with predictions from Stage 4a)
  - For each mine-month combination, formulates and solves a Mixed-Integer
    Linear Programming (MILP) problem using Google OR-Tools (SCIP solver):

    DECISION VARIABLES:
      x[d,s] in {0,1} — whether dumper d is assigned to shovel s
      y[s] in [0, shovel_cap] — throughput of shovel s (continuous)

    CONSTRAINTS:
      - Each available dumper assigned to exactly 1 shovel
      - Unavailable dumpers assigned to 0 shovels
      - Maximum 4 dumpers per shovel (prevents queuing)
      - y[s] <= sum of assigned dumper effective capacities (flow limit)
      - y[s] <= shovel_capacity (shovel limit)
      - Total daily throughput <= crusher capacity (bottleneck)

    PENALTIES:
      - Weather: if rainfall > 100mm, penalty = (rainfall/500) x 30%
      - Road: penalty = (5 - road_condition) x 10%
      - Combined penalty reduces effective dumper capacity

    OBJECTIVE: Maximize total daily throughput
      = Sum of y[s] x operating_hours (16 hours/day, 2 shifts x 8 hours)

  - Generates automated alerts:
      CRITICAL: achievable throughput < 90% of planned
      WARNING: heavy rainfall expected, poor haul road condition
      INFO: dumper unavailable for maintenance

OUTPUTS: data/dispatch_plan.csv + data/fleet_alerts.csv

WHY IT IS IMPORTANT:
  This is where AI meets operations research. The MILP formulation guarantees
  an OPTIMAL assignment — not just a good one. Unlike heuristic approaches
  (e.g., "assign each dumper to the nearest shovel"), MILP explores all possible
  assignments and finds the one that maximizes throughput while respecting every
  constraint simultaneously. This is critical in a mine where a single sub-
  optimal assignment cascades: a shovel starved of dumpers idles (Rs 10,000+/hour
  wasted), while another shovel has dumpers queuing (wasted diesel, driver time).
  The alert system transforms raw optimization output into actionable intelligence
  that mine managers can immediately act on.

3.12  src/pages/07_explainability.py — AI Explainability / XAI (DNA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  - Uses the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) library for model
    interpretability
  - Has 2 tabs: GeoProspect AI (prospectivity) and MineFlow (production)
  - For EACH model:
    GLOBAL EXPLANATIONS:
      - SHAP Beeswarm Plot: shows how each feature pushes predictions up/down
        across all samples. Each dot is one data point. Color = feature value,
        position = SHAP value (impact on prediction)
      - Mean |SHAP| Importance: bar chart showing the average absolute impact
        of each feature on predictions
    LOCAL EXPLANATIONS:
      - User selects a specific grid point (prospectivity) or mine-month
        (production)
      - SHAP Waterfall Plot: shows exactly which features pushed that specific
        prediction up or down, and by how much
      - Natural language explanation: "This location scored 0.87 (High) because:
        iron_oxide_index=2.34 (contributed +0.15), fault_distance_km=3.2
        (contributed +0.12), clay_index=1.89 (contributed +0.08)..."
      - Feature values table for the selected point

WHY IT IS IMPORTANT:
  AI explainability is CRITICAL for adoption in the mining industry. Geologists
  and mine managers will NOT trust a black-box AI that says "drill here" without
  explaining why. SHAP provides mathematically rigorous explanations grounded in
  game theory (Shapley values). The global plots show the overall behavior of
  the model — "Iron Oxide Index is the most important feature" builds confidence
  that the model has learned geologically meaningful patterns (high iron oxide
  DOES correlate with manganese deposits in reality). The local plots explain
  individual predictions — "this specific location is high-priority because it
  has high iron oxide, is near a fault, and has favorable clay minerals." This
  directly addresses the "trust gap" that prevents AI adoption in traditional
  industries. Without this page, the prototype would be a black box; WITH it,
  every prediction is transparent and auditable.

      Working days per month
      AI-driven shortfall reduction percentage
  - Calculates 5 financial impact areas:

    1. EXPLORATION COST SAVINGS (GeoProspect AI):
       Compares 100 traditional blind drill sites vs AI-targeted sites
       Savings = avoided_sites x drill_cost_per_site

    2. PRODUCTION REVENUE PROTECTION (MineFlow):
       Calculates historic revenue loss from production shortfalls
       AI recovery = total_loss x shortfall_reduction_percentage
       Annualized recovery projection

    3. FLEET OPTIMIZATION SAVINGS:
       Reduces idle dumper costs (15% route optimization improvement)
       Diesel savings from optimized routes
       Monthly and annual fleet cost savings

    4. ESG & CARBON IMPACT:
       CO2 avoided from diesel savings (2.68 kg CO2/litre)
       Forest saved from avoided exploratory drilling (0.5 hectares/site)
       Equivalent trees preserved (400 trees/hectare)

    5. TOTAL ROI SUMMARY:
       Sum of all annual savings
       System development cost (Rs 50 lakh)
       Break-even period in months

WHY IT IS IMPORTANT:
  Technical excellence alone does not drive adoption — financial justification
  does. This page translates every technical improvement (better prospectivity
  mapping, reduced shortfalls, optimized fleet) into rupees. When a MOIL
  executive sees "the system pays for itself in X months" with adjustable
  assumptions, the business case writes itself. The ESG section addresses a
  growing regulatory and investor concern — mining companies face increasing
  pressure to demonstrate environmental responsibility. Quantifying CO2
  reductions and forest preservation provides ESG reporting data. The adjustable
  sliders let stakeholders plug in their own numbers, preventing the "those
  aren't realistic assumptions" objection.


3.15  src/pages/05_methodology.py — Data & Model Provenance (Microscope)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IT DOES:
  - Documents all data sources in a table with Source, Type (REAL vs SYNTHETIC),
    and Purpose
  - Checks if real spectral data exists and labels accordingly
  - Details GeoProspect AI architecture: PU Bagging RF, Spatial Block CV, NDVI

  - Displays feature importance chart and confusion matrix
  - Details MineFlow Optimizer: Gradient Boosting Regressor, MILP Fleet Dispatch

  - Lists all research references
  - Documents reproducibility: random_state=42, directory structure, staged

WHY IT IS IMPORTANT:
  Transparency and reproducibility are non-negotiable in a hackathon and in any
  production AI system. This page serves as the "scientific methods section" —
  it tells judges and stakeholders EXACTLY what data was used, where it came
  from, whether it's real or synthetic, what models were trained, how they were
  validated, and how to reproduce the results. The real-vs-synthetic distinction
  is prominently displayed because intellectual honesty about data provenance
  is critical. The research references demonstrate that the approaches used
  (PU learning, spatial CV, spectral indices for mineral mapping) are grounded
  in peer-reviewed science, not invented ad hoc.
